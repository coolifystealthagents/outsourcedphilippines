# Sales Lead Routing SLAs for Philippines-Based Support

An inbound lead can be technically assigned and still go nowhere. The territory field may be blank, two representatives may believe the other owns the account, or an automatic clock may start while the message is trapped in spam review. A response-time target does not fix those problems on its own. The routing lane needs a precise start event, a named owner, and a visible exception path.

Philippines-based sales support can monitor intake, verify defined fields, apply approved routing rules, and escalate unowned records. The role should not infer buying intent, invent qualification evidence, or send claims that sales and legal owners have not approved.

## Choose the event that starts the clock

"Respond within one hour" is ambiguous until the team defines what counts as receipt. A web form submission, accepted API event, manual import, and forwarded email reach the system in different ways. Pick the authoritative timestamp for each channel. Preserve the raw receipt time and record any later time when the item becomes eligible for routing.

Eligibility rules should cover obvious failures. A test submission, security scan, duplicate, malformed address, or message unrelated to sales may enter the intake system without becoming a sales lead. Do not delete these records to protect the SLA. Mark the disposition and retain enough evidence for a reviewer to see why the item was excluded.

The clock also needs a stop event. Assignment is not the same as acceptance, and an automated email is not always a meaningful response. Decide whether the target measures first human review, approved outreach, owner acceptance, or another observable event. Use separate measures when the business needs more than one.

## Route from evidence rather than intuition

Write the routing table with fields that staff can verify: stated country, selected product, existing account identifier, submitted language, partner code, or another approved attribute. For each field, name the controlling source. Free-text comments can add context, but they should not silently override an account record or territory policy.

A support specialist may notice that a message sounds urgent or promising. That impression is not a routing rule. If commercial priority depends on deal size, customer status, or fit, the accountable sales owner must define how those facts are established. The support lane can flag missing evidence and send the record to a review queue.

Keep a rule version with each assignment. When territories change, a manager should be able to explain why yesterday's lead went to a different owner than today's similar lead. Editing the table in place without history makes disputes hard to resolve and weakens performance reporting.

## Handle duplicates without erasing the second signal

Duplicate detection is useful, but a repeated contact is not necessarily noise. A buyer may submit a second form because the first response failed, use a different email address, or ask about a separate product. Define the comparison fields and time window, then distinguish an exact technical replay from a possible related record.

For a possible duplicate, link the records and show the earlier owner and status. Do not merge them automatically if doing so could overwrite a new request or consent state. The sales owner can decide whether to continue one conversation, create a separate opportunity, or suppress an invalid record.

Suppose a contact submits a pricing question on Monday and an implementation question on Wednesday. The email address matches, but the second message names a different business unit. The coordinator attaches the earlier record and routes the new question under the approved account rule. Treating it as a duplicate and closing it would hide information the owner needs.

## Create a real unowned queue

Every rule table has gaps. New regions appear, owners take leave, integrations omit fields, and named accounts change hands. The recovery design matters more than pretending exceptions will disappear.

An unowned record should show the raw source, received time, fields used, failed or conflicting rule, proposed owner if one can be identified, and escalation deadline. Give the queue a person who is responsible for resolving routing policy, not merely a shared inbox. If the deadline passes, escalate without restarting the age.

Avoid temporary assignment to whoever is online unless the policy explicitly permits it. That shortcut can create competing outreach and obscure account ownership. A safe hold with fast escalation is better than an unsupported assignment that appears compliant on a dashboard.

## Separate routing from outreach permission

An assigned record is not automatic permission to use every channel. Outreach must follow the company's approved basis, consent and suppression rules, message claims, and channel-specific requirements. The support specialist can check required fields and suppression evidence before preparing an approved sequence. The responsible owner decides the lawful and appropriate contact strategy.

The Federal Trade Commission's CAN-SPAM compliance guide explains requirements for commercial email, including accurate header information, non-deceptive subject lines, identification, a postal address, and a working opt-out method. It does not cover every jurisdiction or every channel. A routing workflow should point to the company's current policy rather than asking an assistant to interpret a broad regulation for each lead.

If a recipient opts out, verify that the status reaches the systems used for future selection. Closing the request in one inbox while leaving the contact eligible in another tool is not completion.

## Audit a day in sequence

Sample a contiguous period instead of selecting only clean records. Reconstruct receipt, eligibility, assignment, acceptance, first approved response, reassignment, and final disposition. Check excluded items and unowned exceptions as well as successful routes.

Report the eligible population, median and tail times, exception counts by reason, records reassigned, duplicate candidates, and responses sent after suppression. Do not present an average without the slow cases. A low median can coexist with a small set of leads that receive no owner at all.

Begin with one channel and one routing table. Test a normal lead, an existing account, a possible duplicate, a missing territory, an absent owner, and an opt-out conflict.
