# Month-End Close Support Checklist for a Philippines-Based Team

Month-end close does not become easier just because the recurring tasks are written in a spreadsheet. A checklist can hide late source files, stale account mappings, unexplained differences, and approvals carried forward from last month. The useful question is whether each step leaves evidence that a reviewer can inspect.

A Philippines-based bookkeeping support role can collect source records, prepare defined comparisons, maintain the exception queue, and assemble a review pack. It should not choose accounting treatment, set materiality, approve journal entries, release payments, or declare the books closed. The checklist needs to show where preparation ends and professional judgment begins.

## Start with a close calendar that names inputs

List every recurring deliverable with its source, expected availability, preparer, reviewer, and cutoff. "Bank reconciliation" is too broad. A workable entry names the relevant accounts, statement source, ledger cutoff, treatment of pending transactions, output location, and person who resolves unmatched items.

Separate the date an input should arrive from the date the review must finish. When those dates share one checkbox, a late file can appear on time after the preparer rushes the comparison. Keep the original due time and actual receipt time. This lets the finance owner see whether close pressure comes from collection, preparation, or review.

Dependencies also belong in the calendar. Revenue review may depend on an approved billing export. Accrual preparation may depend on purchase records and owner estimates. Do not mark a dependent task late without showing the missing upstream decision. A visible dependency is more useful than a red status with no recovery path.

## Preserve the source before transforming it

Close support often begins with downloads and exports. Save the source identifier, reporting period, retrieval time, currency, and filter conditions before cleaning or reshaping a file. If the source can change after download, keep a protected copy or a reproducible retrieval reference under the company's record policy.

The working file should not erase unusual records simply because they break a formula. Put rejected rows, duplicates, missing identifiers, and format errors into an exception tab or queue. Record the rule that caught each item. A reviewer should be able to trace a total back through the transformation to the original source.

Be careful with convenient substitutes. A figure in last month's workbook may help with comparison, but it is not this month's source. An emailed screenshot may explain a balance, but it does not automatically replace a system report. When the expected source is unavailable, hold the task or use the documented contingency approved by finance.

## Prepare reconciliations without deciding the answer

For each reconciliation, define the two populations being compared, the match keys, timing tolerance, treatment of duplicates, and acceptable difference. These rules should come from the finance owner. Support staff can apply them consistently and show what remains unmatched.

Suppose a bank line and ledger entry share an amount but have different dates and descriptions. The worker can identify the candidate match and attach both records. The worker should not force the match merely to reduce the open count. Likewise, an old reconciling item should retain its original age even if it is copied into a new monthly file.

Use clear states such as matched by rule, candidate requiring review, missing from ledger, missing from source, timing item, duplicate candidate, and owner explanation pending. If finance approves an adjustment, link the decision and the resulting entry. Keep the preparation record distinct from the approval and posting evidence.

## Build the exception queue around decisions

A close exception needs more than a description. Show the account or process, observed difference, amount and currency when relevant, source links, first observed date, prior related item, current owner, decision required, and close impact. This makes escalation useful without asking the owner to repeat the preparer's work.

Group exceptions by the person who can resolve them. Missing vendor documentation may go to accounts payable. An unexplained customer balance may need accounts receivable input. Classification and accounting treatment belong to finance. System access or export failures go to the application owner. A queue organized only by account number can scatter one owner's decisions across several files.

Do not convert silence into approval. If an owner misses the response time, escalate through the defined route and show the consequence. The item remains open until an authorized decision or documented close treatment exists. Moving it to next month without that record hides aging and weakens the next review.

## Assemble a review pack that answers obvious questions

The reviewer should receive the source references, preparation date, applied rules, comparison totals, open exceptions, prior-month carryovers, proposed entries awaiting approval, and evidence for completed corrections. Use summaries to guide attention, but retain the underlying detail.

Variance commentary needs attribution. A support specialist can state that an expense increased by a measured amount and attach the underlying records. The specialist should not invent a business explanation. If an operating owner supplies the explanation, record who provided it and when. If the reason remains unknown, say so plainly and route the question.

Keep reviewer comments with the version they address. Updating a workbook in place after review can make the approval appear to cover changes the reviewer never saw. Use version identifiers or controlled history. If a material change occurs after sign-off, send the affected section back through review instead of relying on the earlier approval.

## Verify posting and close status separately

An approved entry is not proof of a posted entry. After the authorized person posts a correction, compare the destination system with the approved amount, account, period, and reference. Record the system evidence and the person who performed the check. The support role can verify deterministic fields without becoming the approver.

The same rule applies to the close itself. Completing all preparation tasks does not authorize the statement that the period is closed. Finance should own the final close decision, including any accepted open items. Preserve the list of exceptions that remain and the plan for each one.

Reopened items are useful evidence. If a reconciliation must be redone because a source changed or an entry was reversed, link the new work to the original close record. Do not overwrite history to make the checklist look clean.

## Review performance after the pressure drops

Track source arrival time, preparation cycle time, reviewer response time, exception age, first-pass acceptance, reopened tasks, manual adjustments, and recurring causes. Counts need a denominator. Five returned reconciliations mean something different in a population of ten than in a population of two hundred.

Review exceptions after close while the context is still available. Ask whether the source definition, match rule, access, owner map, example, or cutoff should change. Fixing the workflow is more valuable than asking staff to work faster around the same ambiguity next month.

The U.S. Government Accountability Office's Standards for Internal Control discusses documentation, quality information, control activities, and segregation of duties. It can inform workflow design, but it does not make a private company a federal entity or settle its accounting obligations. The company's qualified finance owners must set the actual accounting policy and approval rules.

Begin with one account group and one complete close cycle. Confirm that source records are traceable, exceptions keep their age, approvals are attributable, and posted results match authorized decisions.
